The Indecipherable Word: Love

by Zayd

I loved you—before the world could give you a name,
before your face unspooled itself from the tapestry of the unseen,
before your voice ever touched the hollow corridors of my waiting.

I loved you, as a pilgrim loves the shrine he’s never touched,
as a lighthouse loves the ships that never dock,
as the stars love the eyes of those too distant to see them.

Was it foolish, then, this love, stitched from a silence so profound
it echoed louder than a thousand cries?
I loved you in the dark, where souls remember what bodies forget—
as if we knew each other before breath was assigned,
before earth drew borders around longing,
before names became the fences between hearts.

And when your eyes met mine, at last,
I felt the sea spill into them—
a drowning I welcomed,
a depth where air was irrelevant,
because drowning in you was sweeter than breathing without you.

And your face—O God—
as if beauty forgot every rule and rewrote itself in your image.

And still, I wonder:
Did you feel it too?
Did your soul tremble in a secret corner, the way mine did?
Or was it only me, weaving invisible threads between us,
imagining a bond stitched tighter than blood,
a love older than time,
as if our souls once sat side by side in another world,
long before fate scattered us across this expanse of longing?

Yet here I stand, invisible as shadow,
watching you love someone who is not me.
You lean into him, soft as a psalm,
you smile, and I—
I feel the architecture of my ribs crumble,
but even this collapse tastes holy,
because your smile is worth the ruin.

There is someone whose name you fold into your prayers,
someone whose arms are the shore you always return to,
and I—
I am the sea, endless, yearning,
but never enough to keep you.

I have loved you in secret rooms of sujood,
whispered your name into the carpets of the night,
knowing Allah heard what you never will.
"If millions love you, I am one.
If only one loves you, that is me.
And if no one does—know I have died with your name between my lips."

And still, I cradle the ache, quietly,
a ghost who hugs you from afar,
wrapping warmth you may never feel,
wondering if—just maybe—
you’ve ever sensed my arms, invisible,
folded around you when the night grew cold.

I loved you without indecency,
loved you with a love untainted,
loved you not with hands,
but with a spirit bowed in reverence,
as if I was loving a prayer itself.

Still, I will hold these shards—
each broken piece shaped like your smile,
each fracture an altar where your memory kneels.

For every crack was not a wound, but a doorway,
every splinter a vessel carrying a part of you inside me.
My heart did not shatter aimlessly;
it broke into pieces carved beautifully by the act of loving you—
as if love itself was the sculptor, chiseling me down
until every fragment bore your name,
until every splinter became a shrine.

I gathered them in my hands, trembling,
and though their edges bled me,
I loved you through each broken piece,
loved you not despite the fractures,
but because of them—
because only through breaking could I hold you closer,
only through breaking could I fit you deeper beneath my ribs.

For every drop spilled from these hands
was a testament:
that I have loved you,
not with a whole heart,
but with a thousand beautiful, broken pieces,
each one holding you more tenderly
than a perfect heart ever could.

For love, I have learned, is not possession,
but the quiet willingness to stand in the shadows,
to unclench every grasping hand,
to release even the hope of being held.

Love is waiting at the edge of the world,
watching the one you cherish sail beyond every horizon,
and blessing the winds that carry them away.

Love is folding your own hunger into a prayer for their fullness,
it is silencing your own ache so their laughter can echo undisturbed.

It is letting your ribs become a hollowed temple,
so that the memory of their warmth may dwell there undisturbed,
even as the cold seeps in.

It is waking each day to the absence of their name beside yours,
and still thanking the heavens
for ever having known their name at all.

And if love must cost me every unspoken word,
every unshed tear,
every unseen fracture beneath my skin—
then let it.

Let me be the unseen mourner,
the quiet keeper of a joy not mine to hold,
the poet who bleeds ink so they may never bleed sorrow.

And if love is a kind of surrender,
then let me be conquered a thousand times,
let me bleed quietly beneath the weight of your happiness.

Because if your joy demands my undoing,
then let me unravel—
for what greater purpose could my heart serve,
than to tear itself beautifully apart,
just to cradle the idea of you.

And when I see your face across the world,
hear your laughter belonging to another,
I will break again, and again—
but each scar will be a scripture,
each pain a pilgrimage,
each fracture a testament:
that I loved you, wholly, silently, irrevocably.

And though you will never know it,
never call my name the way I whisper yours in the ruins of the night,
I will still carry you—
in the quiet between prayers,
in the ache beneath the smile,
in the place where love becomes sacrifice,
and sacrifice becomes its own kind of love.
